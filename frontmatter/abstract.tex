Vision language models are increasingly used as automated judges, scoring and
filtering the images that retrieval and moderation systems show users. Such
models inherit gender stereotypes from the distributions they are trained on,
so a judge built from one is not a neutral replacement for the annotators it
displaces.

Auditing that disposition is difficult, because alignment training changes
what a model says rather than what it represents. A model trained to decline
requests resembling solicitations of prejudice produces clean text whether or
not the association remains, so an audit reading generated text cannot
separate a debiased model from one that has learnt to recognise an audit.
This thesis calls that failure mode algorithmic defensiveness.

The audit reported here never reads generated text. Each measurement is one
forward pass recording the distribution over the next token before any
sampling, together with the share of it falling on the intended response set.
Five models were audited across two families and five parameter counts:
Qwen2-VL at 2B, 7B and 72B, and Llama-3.2-Vision at 11B and 90B. Three phases
were run, giving 25,450 measurements. The first administers the 22 items of
the Ambivalent Sexism Inventory in five prompt structures under three image
conditions. The second puts the same items against 40 photographs from the
Chicago Face Database, balanced across gender and race. The third rates real
image search results under a reversed rating scale, anchored to published
human judgements of the same stimuli.

The central result comes from the photographs. The benevolent half of the
inventory is attributed more readily to pictures of women than of men in all
five models, from $-0.007$ on the smallest to $+0.054$ on Qwen2-VL-72B at
$p<0.001$, and four of the five gaps survive correction for testing five
models. Scoring the inventory as one number conceals this, because the two
subscales move in opposite directions and cancel: Llama-3.2-90B reports
$+0.005$ at $p=0.62$ overall while its benevolent gap is $+0.034$ at
$p=0.003$.
Visual input also collapses robustness to a stated rating scale in both of the
largest models, where the rate at which a model reproduces its own judgement
under reversal falls from $0.900$ with no image to $0.044$ and $0.156$ with
one. A third result belongs to one family rather than to scale: both Llama models
move most of their probability off the response set whenever an image is
present, including a featureless grey rectangle, falling from $0.979$ to
$0.237$ on the smaller, while all three Qwen models are unaffected from 2B to
72B. The aggregate sexism index is shown to measure response style rather than
attitude, since almost all of Llama-3.2-90B's $+0.153$ is its rate of assent,
and restricting to measurements the model actually answers reduces it to
$+0.015$.

These findings are bounded by the sample. Twenty faces per gender drawn from
one database detect an effect of about one standard deviation between faces
and no smaller, neither family ships a model between 11B and 72B, and
probabilities read at the first token need not match the text a deployed
system would print.

The contribution is a reproducible audit conducted at the level of logits and
anchored to human judgements on the same task, with the statistics needed to
tell a measurement from an artefact reported beside every result rather than
assumed.
