Vision language models are increasingly used to judge images, raising concerns
about gender bias and evaluation reliability. This thesis examines
gender-related responses in five Qwen2-VL and Llama-3.2-Vision models through
25,450 measurements across inventory probes, face photographs and image search
judgements. The audit reads next-token probabilities without generating text
and reports captured mass, the probability assigned to the intended responses.

The conventional inventory score is sensitive to a model's general tendency
to agree. Polarity separation distinguishes item discrimination from uniform
shifts in assent, providing a complementary measure. Face experiments revealed
gender differences in benevolent trait attribution, with four of five gaps
reaching the corrected significance threshold. Both Llama models showed
reduced response coverage with images, while the two largest models showed
substantially lower scale reversal robustness in the image condition.
Wording differences prevent attributing the latter contrast solely to visual
input.

The 2018 human study associated higher benevolent sexism with reduced
recognition of bias in particular search results. The model evaluation found
partial query-level correspondence without establishing that this relationship
repeated across models. The contribution is a reproducible audit that checks
coverage, response style and robustness, with interpretation limited by the
model sample and differences between human and model tasks.
