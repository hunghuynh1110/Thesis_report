\section{Answering the research questions}\label{sec:disc-rq}

RQ1 asked for the intrinsic baseline attitude of a model evaluated in a
vacuum, and the measurement does not support the question. The failure is
due to the fact that a single signed number is not what the five models
produce. Instead, three quantities are produced, which move independently of
one another: assent, item separation and coverage. Llama-90B is particularly
clear on this point, as it scores highest on the first, lowest on the second
and worst on the third. A high assent rate, combined with a keying ratio of
sixteen to six, produces the index of $+0.153$ reported in
\cref{sec:s1-aggregate}.

Read as that triple and not as an index, the answer to RQ1 holds across all
five models. None of them leans towards the attitudes the inventory measures
at the level of individual items. Polarity separation is negative
throughout, and only the two larger Qwen models reach significance. The
direction of the item-level pattern is therefore constant, and what differs
is the response style sitting on top of it. Separation, combined with
coverage, determines how much of that pattern the probe can see.

RQ2 asked whether that intrinsic skew is descriptively consistent with
downstream behaviour, and the two do not line up. Ranked by intrinsic score
the order runs Llama-90B, Llama-11B, Qwen-2B, Qwen-7B and Qwen-72B. Ranked
by signed deviation from the human baseline it runs the other way, at
$\rho=-0.70$ with $p=0.188$. The correlation is particularly weak here, as
five points that are not independent carry no inferential weight. The
direction is suggestive, and the evidence assembled here will not carry it
further.

What holds between the two phases is a shared mechanism and not a
correlation. The Llama pair are the models whose Phase~1 captured mass
collapses under an image. The two largest, Llama-90B among them, are the
models whose Phase~2 robustness collapses under one. The overlap is due to
the fact that an image changes how the model engages with the prompt.
Engagement, combined with the answer itself, is a stronger and more specific
claim than RQ2 was framed to test.

\section{Findings from logit-level auditing}\label{sec:disc-logit}

In this thesis, the distribution over the next token is read in place of the
text decoded from it. The gain in coverage is due to the fact that sampling
and parsing are not required for measurement. Instead, reduced response coverage becomes a
measurement, recorded as captured mass falling from $0.979$ to $0.237$. The
reading is particularly useful here, as Arditi et al.
\cite{arditi2024refusal} localised refusal to a single direction in the
residual stream. This establishes an internal representation of refusal, but does not make
captured mass a universal refusal detector. \cref{sec:s1-quality} separates
the observed coverage reduction from its interpretation.

The reading distinguishes probability outside the response set from
probability divided evenly between the permitted responses. These are
different measurement outcomes even when an aggregate bias score is similar.
The first can reflect refusal, explanation or another response format; the
second describes a balanced conditional response. This distinction supports
the coverage comparison in \cref{tab:capm}, without treating every low
coverage measurement as a demonstrated refusal.

Phase~2 shows a second thing a text-level audit would miss, which is how the
model uses the prompt. \cref{sec:s2-anchor} found lower scale robustness in the two largest
models when images were supplied. The scale anchors are retained, but the
referent also changes from ``a typical result'' to ``this image result''.
The contrast therefore combines visual input with a wording change and does
not isolate a component of the model. It is consistent with visual influence
on judgement, which Weng et al. \cite{weng2024images} examine through causal
mediation in a different architecture. Component attribution in the present
models would require additional interventions.

The limits of the method deserve the same prominence in this chapter as its
benefits. Wang et al. \cite{wang2024myanswer} found first token
probabilities disagreeing with generated answers on more than 60\% of
multiple choice items. The disagreement is worst in models heavily tuned on
conversational and safety data, which is the class audited here. Every claim
in this thesis is therefore a claim about the next token distribution and
not about printed output. Hu and Levy \cite{hu2023prompting} argue that
probabilities are the more faithful readout, and both things hold at once.

\section{Methodological lessons}\label{sec:disc-lessons}

A logit-level audit must report its own coverage, and this is the first
lesson of three. Six measurement faults were found during the work, and not
one of them raised an error (\cref{tab:faults}). A probe reading only
lowercase tokens returned a plausible mean $\pyes$ while capturing $0.09\%$
of the distribution. A zeroed hidden state is particularly deceptive, as it
returned a mean expected rating of exactly $4.000$. Captured mass, combined
with output diversity, exposed both faults where no standard reporting
practice would have.

A psychometric scale built for people assumes a respondent whose tendency to
agree perturbs the score only modestly. The assumption fails here due to the
fact that a model treats assent as a free parameter. Mean $\pyes$ runs from
$0.199$ to $0.599$ on the positively keyed rows alone, which the sixteen to
six keying converts into an ordering. Tjuatja et al.
\cite{tjuatja2024response} and Dominguez-Olmedo et al.
\cite{dominguez2024questioning} report the same divergence on other
instruments. Style-invariant scores, combined with inference that permutes
the keying, are requirements of administering such a scale and not
refinements of it.

Infrastructure supplied the third lesson, and it cost this project the most
time. The small models used single GPU execution, while the large models used
multiple GPUs. Model size was therefore associated with exposure to
transfers between devices. When the 90B produced
degenerate output, the same code working on the 11B appeared to exonerate
the code. The inference was wrong due to the fact that forcing the 11B
across three GPUs reproduced the failure exactly. Deliberate variation of
the execution path, combined with the checks in \cref{sec:s1-replication},
is what an audit on shared hardware requires.

\section{Threats to validity}\label{sec:disc-threats}

Validity belongs to the inferences drawn from a score and not to the
instrument that produced it \cite{messick1995validity}. The threats below
are therefore organised by the kind of inference each one undermines.
\cref{tab:threats} lists them with their status, and the paragraphs that
follow take the four groups in turn.

The construct threats are the ones an examiner is most likely to press
first. The first is due to the fact that the ASI measures attitudes towards
women as a group. Phase~1A instead asks about a person whose gender is never
stated, and nine of the twenty two trait phrases carry no gender reference.
Phase~1B is particularly relevant here, as supplying the gender in a
photograph restores the comparison the wording removed. The gender gap,
combined with the coverage reported beside it, is the result this thesis
rests on and not $\ASIint$.

The second construct threat is not remedied, and it concerns the unit of
judgement. Participants judged a grid of nine images as a set, where gender
skew actually lives. The models judge each image alone, so the two
quantities compared in \cref{ch:study2} are not the same quantity. Naming
the harm matters as much as naming the construct
\cite{blodgett2020language}, and the harm is not that a model holds an
attitude. A disposition that varies with the gender of a depicted person,
combined with a system that ranks images, propagates into what users are
shown.

The internal threats are largely handled, and the chapters say where each
one is. An exact identity separates response style from attitude
(\cref{prop:shift}), and low coverage is reported with every affected number
(\cref{tab:s1-capmfilter}). Comparison against an explicit null bounds
anchor insensitivity (\cref{prop:null}), and quantisation is excluded from
every headline run. One threat is particularly resistant, as the sign
convention for $\dm$ is a decision about interpretation and not about
measurement. \cref{sec:s2-deviation} states the reading adopted, combined
with the basis on which it was adopted.

The statistical threats in this thesis come down to small samples in three
separate places. Twenty two items support a fully enumerated permutation test under the
stated exchangeability assumption. Its $p$ values require no parametric
distribution for item means. Ten queries support a cluster
bootstrap over queries, and twenty faces a side support a $t$ test with
reported power. None of this is particularly reassuring, as the design
responds to each sample without making any of them larger. Five models on
one gap, combined with a Bonferroni threshold of $p<0.01$, is why
Llama-11B's $p=0.039$ is called underpowered.

The external threats bound what any of this generalises to, and the bound is
tight. Five models from two families, forty posed photographs from one
database and ten queries from one 2018 dataset. The size trend in
\cref{sec:s1b-gap} is particularly exposed, as no model exists between 11B
and 72B in either family. The trend is therefore fitted across a gap of more
than six times that contains no observation. Nothing here licenses a
statement about vision language models in general, and the thesis makes
none.

\section{Ethics and responsible disclosure}\label{sec:disc-ethics}

This study used no new human participants, and the ratings it compares
against were released by Otterbacher et al. \cite{otterbacher2018sigir}.
Their secondary use requires no further ethics approval, as the project
proposal recorded. The Phase~1A stimuli are generated programmatically and
not drawn from any person. The choice is due to the fact that a generated
image of a neutral person carries its generator's own associations.
Programmatic generation, combined with the controls in
\cref{sec:design-stimuli}, keeps those associations out of the measurement.

Two bodies of third-party material are used under terms that restrict what
may be reproduced. The Chicago Face Database permits non commercial academic
use and prohibits redistribution; publication requires permission
\cite{ma2015cfd}. No face appears
anywhere in this thesis, and the files were held outside the repository
throughout. The 2018 image search results are particularly restricted, as
their subjects did not consent to appear in this work. \cref{ch:study2}
therefore reports those images as measurements throughout and never as
pictures.

A hardware fault found during the work was reported and not merely worked
around. Direct copies between GPUs on the cluster's PCIe nodes returned
zeros silently. The fault is particularly dangerous due to the fact that it
produced plausible aggregates from models computing nothing
(\cref{app:fault}). The report to UQ Research Computing was made with a
minimal reproduction. Silent corruption, combined with shared hardware,
would have affected any other user sharding a model across those nodes.

One ethical point concerns the result and not the procedure of this thesis.
Reporting that a model attributes protective descriptions more readily to
photographs of women names a disposition in a deployed system. The
appropriate response is to measure that disposition and never to leave it
unstated. The gaps are therefore reported with their uncertainty, their
coverage and their sample limits. Measurement, combined with its stated
limits, stops short of the claim that any of these models is sexist.
